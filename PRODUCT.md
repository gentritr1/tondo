# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Casual groups of two to four friends or family members playing a quick card game together on desktop or mobile. Players may already understand UNO-style matching, but the interface must also make each legal action and special rule understandable without a tutorial or rules lookup.

## Product Purpose

Tondo is a lightweight multiplayer pizza card game built around a shared round table. It should get a group from name entry to active play quickly, make the current turn and legal next move unmistakable, and turn familiar matching-card mechanics into a friendly, memorable social game.

## Brand Personality

Playful, confident, and welcoming. Tondo should feel like a lively neighborhood game night: colorful and expressive when something happens, calm and focused while a player decides what to do.

## Anti-references

Avoid casino-style spectacle, dense competitive-game HUDs, generic neon gaming dashboards, ornamental glass panels, and interfaces that rely on color alone. Avoid showing persistent instructions, scores, or controls that are not relevant to the current decision.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next move obvious: the active player, playable cards, and one primary action should be recognizable at a glance.
2. Reveal rules in context: explain TONDO, callouts, wild choices, and drawn-card decisions only when they become actionable.
3. Keep the table social: opponents, turn order, and recent play should be present without competing with the player's hand.
4. Spend motion on meaning: use quick, tactile animation for card play, draws, turn changes, and wins; keep idle surfaces quiet.
5. Preserve a one-screen play loop: the core game should stay usable without vertical page scrolling on common phones and laptops.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for text and controls. Use labels, shapes, values, and icons in addition to topping colors; maintain visible keyboard focus and 44px touch targets; announce turn and game events to assistive technology; and provide reduced-motion behavior that preserves state changes without movement.
